## Diapositiva 4

4 PUBLIC Business Example 1 – Adding a weight unit of measure to a group OEC is changing suppliers for cables. The new supplier sells cables by weight. OEC will purchase cables in kilograms (kg) and manage them in inventory by meters.  1 meter Network cable  =  1 kg  1 meter of HDMI cable  =  2 kg  1 meter of Power cable =  4 kg We will change an existing unit of measure group to support purchasing these items by weight while selling the items in meters. OEC computers resells different types of cables. A unit of measure group already exists for this item with meter as the base unit of measure. Previously the cables were purchased in 100 meter reels.  OEC has decided to change to a new supplier who sells the cables by weight. OEC will purchase cables by weight and manage them in inventory by meters. These cables will be sold in various units that convert to meters. Because cables are purchased by weight and sold by lengths in meters, the unit of measure group of cables must be able to convert quantities in kilograms into quantities in meters Here are 3 examples of cable items managed in OEC Computers : Network cable, HDMI cable  and Power cable. A meter inventory UoM is defined for each item. Each meter unit has a different weight: •1 meter of network cable weighs 1 kg, •1 meter of HDMI cable weighs 2 kg and •1 meter of industrial power cable weighs 4 kg. We will look at how we can change an existing unit of measure group to support purchasing these items by weight while storing and selling the items in meters. 4

## Diapositiva 6

6 PUBLIC Advantage of Weight Factor Group Definition – Cable - Setup 1            1                    Meter                                            =               1                             Meter 4            1                    Kilo                            kg               =               1                             Meter #         Alt Qty Alt UoM Weight Factor      =          Base Qty Base UoM Network Cables  1 meter = 1 kg HDMI Cables      1 meter = 2 kg Power cables      1 meter = 3 kg “Alt Qty” x “Weight Factor “(from Item Master Weight) = Base Qty in Document Use the weight specified in the item master record for the conversion. Let’s talk for a moment of the advantage of using a weight factor in situations where we want to convert quantities in weights to quantities in lengths. In previous releases, we did not have a weight factor and it was much more difficult to manage items that had different weights while having the same length, width or height.  Now with a weight factor we can use the weight listed in the item master record to make the conversion.   This makes it possible to share the same unit of measure group for cable rather than having three separate unit of measure groups. For example, in our company we have 3 general types of cable: network cables, HDMI cables and power cables and they all have different weights.  For network cables, 1 meter equals 1 kilogram.  HDMI cables are twice as heavy so 1 meter equals 2 kilograms.  Power cables are heavier still, with 3 kilograms for each meter. Now we can use the weight factor field to calculate the weight different for the meter unit of measure.  Because we have entered the kilogram weight factor, the system will look at the weight specified in the item master and calculate the actual weight being purchased, sold or inventoried. The system multiplies the alternative quantity by the weight from the item master to calculate the amount of base quantity for a document. 6

## Diapositiva 7

7 PUBLIC Example of how conversion works  The weight factor value comes from the item master weight field.  If the unit differs, a calculation is performed. For example: converting grams to kilograms.  Then calculations are made to convert the Alternative Quantity (Kilo) to the Base Quantity (Meter).  The Weight factor of the network cable = 1 kg  The Weight factor of the HDMI cable = 2 kg  The Weight factor of the power cable = 4 kg  1 Kg of the network cable = 1 meter  1 Kg of the HDMI cable = 0.5 meter  1 Kg of the power cable = 0.25 meter Let’s look at how this works with our business example. The value for the weight factor comes from the value in the Item Master Data Weight field. If the weight of the item was entered in a different weight measurement than the one indicated in the factor field, then the system converts the weight according to the weight factor. For example, if the weight of the item is 2000 grams then the factor will be 2 (kilograms). We have set conversion rules in our unit of measure group.  So calculations are performed to convert the alternative quantity (in our case in kilograms) to the base quantity (in our case meters). Remember the formula Alternative Quantity multiplied by the weight factor equals the base quantity.  Logically this also means that an alternative quantity of a unit equals the base quantity of the unit divided by the weight factor. Therefore the following is true for our cable items. Since we manage the cable inventory by kilograms, we entered the following weights in the Weight field of the Item Master data: 1 kg for the network cable 2 kg for the HDMI cable and 4 kg for the power cable This means that: 1 kilogram unit of the network cable equals 1 meter unit 1 kilogram unit of the HDMI cable equals 0.5 meter unit 1 kilogram unit of the power cable equals 0.25 meter unit 7

Notice how the calculation using the weight factor affects the Item per Unit field.   For example, look at the power cable item.  Since the weight factor of the power cable is 4 kilograms.  If we purchase one kilogram worth of power cable, the length is only a quarter of a meter long. 8

For other items, a weight may need to be a base unit. For example, OEC Computers resells USB connectors.  The USB connectors are bought and stored in kilograms.  However they are sold individually as pieces. The inventory unit is a weight (kilograms).  Therefore, it is a good practice to use the weight as the base unit.  Since the item is sold with a non-weight unit, we need to convert from a weight to a non-weight unit. To do this, we can create a user-defined field to specify the conversion between the base unit in weights and another type of unit, such as pieces. Then we can use the UDF Factor field in the Unit of Measure group to handle the conversion. 9

## Diapositiva 12

12 12 PUBLIC Summary Here are some key points:  Units based on weight can be used in units of measure groups.  A weight factor can be used to convert units of weight to a base unit.  The weight factor value is derived from the Weight field in the Item Master record.  The formula for calculating weight is: Alt Qty” x “Weight Factor “(from Item Master Weight) = Base Qty  SAP Business One has provided a UDF Factor field in the Unit of Measure Group that allows you to include values from a user-defined field to perform additional conversions for units of measure.  The UDF factor allows you to have a weight unit as the base unit of measure in a group. A weight factor can be used to convert units of weight to the base unit. The weight factor value is derived from the Weight field in the Item Master record. When using a weight factor, the formula for the calculating quantities is: “Alternative Quantity” multiplied by the “Weight Factor “(from Item Master Weight) equals the Base Quantity. Additionally Business One has provided a UDF Factor field in the Unit of Measure Group window that allows include values from a user-defined field in the item master to perform additional conversions for units of measure.  For example, a UDF could be used to convert lengths or widths to densities or volumes. The UDF factor also allows you to have a weight unit as the base unit of measure in a group.
